📘 Glossary - new to this? Click to expand
Trailing P/E (Price-to-Earnings)
Current price ÷ earnings over the LAST 12 months. Measures what you're paying per dollar of past profit. High = expensive based on history.
Forward P/E (Fwd P/E)
Current price ÷ earnings analysts EXPECT for the next 12 months. Measures what you're paying based on projected profit. Lower than trailing usually means earnings are expected to grow.
Trailing EPS / Forward EPS
Earnings Per Share. Trailing = last 12 months actual. Forward = next 12 months estimated. Negative EPS = the company is losing money.
Gross Margin
Revenue minus the direct cost of making the product, as a % of revenue. Software companies are usually 70-90%; chip companies 40-70%.
Operating Margin
Profit from core business (after all operating costs) as a % of revenue. Negative = the business runs at a loss before taxes/interest.
EBITA
Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, and Amortization. A profit measure that adds amortization back to operating earnings, but still includes depreciation. Less aggressive than EBITDA because depreciation is not added back.
EBITDA
Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization. A profit measure that adds back both depreciation and amortization, often used to compare operating performance before financing, tax, and major non-cash accounting costs.
FCF (Free Cash Flow)
Cash left after paying for operations and capex. Strongly positive FCF = the company is self-funding and can buy back shares, pay dividends, or invest. The "real money" check on accounting profit.
Market Cap
Total value of all shares = price × shares outstanding. Bigger = larger company, generally less volatile.
Conservative band (red) / Bull band (green)
Scenario prices using P/E methodology: Bull = optimistic P/E × higher EPS; Conservative = compressed P/E × lower EPS. NOT predictions - just a way to size the upside/downside range.
Implied move
Options market's expected % swing on earnings day. Higher = market expects a bigger reaction.
Analyst Mean PT
Average 12-month price target across Wall Street analysts covering the stock. Useful as a sanity check; below current price = market may be pricing in more than the analysts.
DCF Estimate
Discounted Cash Flow estimate. A model that converts expected future cash flow into today's value. Useful for stable cash-generating companies, but very sensitive to assumptions.
Why track this group?

The current AI trend is simple: big tech is still racing to build more compute, but the bottlenecks are physical - chips, HBM memory, advanced packaging, data-center power, cooling, and networking. May to July earnings are the reality check on whether that spending is turning into real orders, backlog, and revenue.

  • What is scarce? GPUs, custom AI chips, HBM memory, advanced packaging, and power capacity decide who can actually build AI systems.
  • What must work? Data centers need networking, servers, cloud capacity, and enough electricity before companies can run bigger AI workloads.
  • What proves demand? Software and cloud names show whether AI is becoming paid products, not just expensive infrastructure spend.
